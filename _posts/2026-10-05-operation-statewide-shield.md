Florida officials say they recovered 163 missing children during a five-day operation called Operation Statewide Shield, which ran from August 17 to 21, 2026. They're describing it as one of the largest child-recovery operations in American history. The effort, led by the Florida Department of Law Enforcement (FDLE), focused on identifying, locating and recovering anyone under 18 who had been reported missing.

The children ranged in age from 2 months to 17 years old, and the FDLE says many of them had been through some level of abuse, neglect, exploitation or exposure to other criminal activity.

The recoveries broke down by region like this:

- Tampa Bay – 46
- Miami – 41
- Jacksonville – 32
- Orlando – 21
- Fort Myers – 12
- Pensacola – 8
- Tallahassee region – 3

Officials said children who needed help were taken by local law enforcement to designated recovery hubs, where they got support from social services, child welfare professionals, medical providers and victim advocates.

The operation also produced criminal cases: the Office of Statewide Prosecution under Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier and the Miami-Dade State Attorney's Office are handling three felony cases that came out of it. Uthmeier said many of these children had been victimized by people who prey on the most vulnerable, and credited FDLE, his own Office of Statewide Prosecution and state and local partners with getting them to safety and into the care they deserve.

The reporting is by Briana Trujillo for NBC 6 South Florida, published August 31, 2026 and updated September 1.
