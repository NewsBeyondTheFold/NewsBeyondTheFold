## What happened

On 10 September 2026, Algeria severed diplomatic relations with the United Arab Emirates, ending five decades of ties. Its foreign ministry summoned UAE ambassador Yousef Saif Khamis Subaa Al-Ali, informed him of the decision, and gave him 48 hours to leave.

The statement said Algeria had "exhausted all means of preserving bilateral relations" and cited "increasingly provocative or hostile actions" from Abu Dhabi. It did not name a single trigger.

The following day, Algeria's defence ministry closed its airspace to all UAE-registered civilian and military aircraft, effective midnight on 11 September. Commercial UAE flights to and from Algiers were exempted until the end of 2026, when the existing contract expires.

The UAE's foreign ministry responded by expressing "appreciation for the brotherly Algerian people" — notably warmer than the language coming the other way.

## Why it happened

Algiers' own statement was deliberately unspecific, and that vagueness is itself the story: the rupture is the product of an accumulation rather than one incident.

The BBC notes the two states sit on opposite sides of the Western Sahara dispute, with the UAE backing Morocco's claim and Algeria supporting the Polisario Front. DW reports Algeria accuses Abu Dhabi of destabilising Libya, Sudan and Mali. Sudan has separately accused the UAE of arming the Rapid Support Forces, which Abu Dhabi denies.

Analysts place more weight on the Sahel. President Tebboune said last year that "wherever there is conflict, this state's money is present — in Mali, in Libya, in Sudan." Middle East Eye reported that Mali's cooling towards Algeria, its traditional ally, has been attributed to Emirati influence. Al-Monitor frames it as a contest over regional alignment running from Western Sahara through the Sahel, against a backdrop of Abu Dhabi's security agreements with Mali and Burkina Faso.

One reading worth flagging, since it's the newest and least corroborated: political scientist Fatiha Dazi-Héni told Channel Africa that energy rivalry was central, pointing to a failed coup attempt in Niger on 20 August, after which Algeria reportedly deployed fighter jets to protect the Trans-Saharan gas pipeline to Europe — with Abu Dhabi funding a competing Atlantic pipeline for Morocco. That's one analyst's account rather than established fact.
